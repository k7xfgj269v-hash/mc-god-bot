A two-story Japanese pagoda with the classic stacked-roof silhouette — wide flared eaves on each level, a ground floor with white shoji-style panels framed in red, and a smaller second story with shadow-darkened lattice screens. The roof tiles use Stripped Acacia Wood and Dark Oak Slabs in striped layers — the orange-and-brown striping mimics traditional Japanese clay tiles aged unevenly by sun. On top sits a small ornamental finial with a red-and-white sōrin (the spire-and-rings ornament that crowns real pagodas), and a tuff-and-bone-block base anchors the building so it looks like it's grown out of stone.

173 steps, with a serious palette: stripped acacia wood (224), dark oak slab (176), spruce slab (172), tuff (108), bone block (90), red and cyan terracotta accents. The bone blocks are doing visual work — they're used as the white wall panels on the upper story to give the shoji a brighter glow than plain white concrete or wool. Drop this in a forest biome with a small bridge and pond and you have one of the most photogenic builds in the catalog. Pair with the Samurai House and Red Japanese Temple for a coherent Edo-era complex.

How to install the Japanese Pagoda schematic in Minecraft

Litematica (survival)

  1. Drop the file in .minecraft/schematics/
  2. Press M → Load Schematic
  3. Place the ghost overlay and build block-by-block

WorldEdit (creative)

  1. Put the file in the server or world's schematics/
  2. Run //schem load japanese-pagoda
  3. Stand at the target and run //paste

Works on Spigot, Paper, Fabric, Forge, and FastAsyncWorldEdit (FAWE). New to Litematica? Read the full Litematica installation & usage guide, or browse the free schematics index.

Builder's tips for the Japanese Pagoda

  • The flared eaves on each roof tier are the signature pagoda detail. They have to angle outward and turn up slightly at the corners — flat eaves make this build collapse into a generic cottage. Use Stripped Acacia Wood placed on its side at the corners for the upturned tip-blocks.
  • Stripe the roof tiles by alternating Stripped Acacia Wood (warm orange) and Dark Oak Slabs (deep brown). Don't use a single color for either tier — the stripe is what reads as terracotta tiling exposed to centuries of weathering.
  • Bone Block (90 of them) is meant for the upper-story shoji panels. It's whiter than concrete, slightly textured, and reads as paper-stretched-on-frame. Substituting white concrete or wool kills the build's soft-glow upper level.
  • The spire (sōrin) on top — red wool/concrete with white pillar segments and small horizontal arms — is small but essential. A flat-topped pagoda doesn't read as a religious building; the finial is the explicit Buddhist signal that anchors the architectural style.
  • The Dead Coral and Dead Tube Coral Block in the materials list (20 + 7) are decorative — they're meant for the rock garden at the base, suggesting weathered stones in a zen layout. Don't skip the garden surround; pagodas in real life are always set in landscaped gardens, never on bare grass.
  • The 24 Oak Buttons are nailheads/door studs. Place them on the wooden parts of the wall where you'd expect joinery. Subtle but it's the kind of detail that pushes the build from "good" to "obviously hand-built by someone who cared."
  • Light the interior of both floors with hidden lanterns or shroomlights so the white bone-block panels glow softly at night. A dark pagoda silhouette against a sunset is iconic; a glowing pagoda at night is the screenshot.

Japanese Pagoda schematic — frequently asked questions

Quick answers on Bedrock support, Minecraft version compatibility, server use, and Litematica setup.

Is the Japanese Pagoda Minecraft schematic free?

Yes. The Japanese Pagoda .schem file is 100% free to download — no signup, no email gate, no watermark, no paywall. You can use it in personal worlds, multiplayer servers, YouTube videos, and streams. See the Terms page for the full license.

Does the Japanese Pagoda schematic work on Minecraft Bedrock Edition?

No. The .schem format is Java Edition only — Bedrock (including Pocket Edition, Windows 10, and console versions) doesn't natively support schematic files or the Litematica mod. Bedrock players should follow the step-by-step Japanese Pagoda tutorial instead, which shows every block placement with images and works on every edition.

What Minecraft version does the Japanese Pagoda schematic support?

The Japanese Pagoda schematic was generated for Minecraft Java Edition 1.21.5, but the Sponge .schem format is backward-compatible with Java 1.18 and above through both Litematica and WorldEdit. If you load it on an older version, unsupported blocks may render as their nearest equivalent.

Can I use the Japanese Pagoda schematic on a Minecraft server?

Yes. WorldEdit works on Spigot, Paper, and Bukkit servers as a plugin. Drop the japanese-pagoda.schem file in the server's plugins/WorldEdit/schematics/ folder and run //schem load japanese-pagoda followed by //paste. For very large builds, FastAsyncWorldEdit (FAWE) is recommended to avoid lag during the paste.

Does Litematica need a .litematic file, or does .schem work?

Litematica reads .schem files natively — no conversion to .litematic is needed. The .schem format is the modern Sponge schematic standard, and Litematica has supported it since version 0.0.12. If you specifically need a .litematic file, you can convert it after loading by opening the schematic menu and selecting "Save schematic" with the .litematic format option.

How many blocks does the Japanese Pagoda build need?

The Japanese Pagoda uses 33 different block types across 173 placement steps. The most common materials are Stripped Acacia Wood and Dark Oak Slab. The full block list with quantities is in the "Block list" section above. Litematica's built-in Material List view also calculates exact totals once the schematic is loaded into your world.

What's the difference between this schematic and the Japanese Pagoda tutorial?

The schematic is a single .schem file you load into Litematica or WorldEdit — fastest way to build it in Java Edition with mods. The Japanese Pagoda tutorial is a step-by-step image guide showing every block placement, which works on any Minecraft edition (Java, Bedrock, Pocket) and doesn't require any mods. Use whichever fits how you play.
